## Big Question

Can God work through ordinary faithful love, even when life feels sad or uncertain?

## Big Truth

God is kind and wise. He cared for Ruth and Naomi through sorrow, loyalty, work, generosity, and family. This is called providence: God’s wise care and rule, even when people cannot see the whole plan yet. God was also preparing a bigger rescue story that would lead to King David and, later, to Jesus.

**Child recap:** Ruth stayed with Naomi. Boaz showed kindness. God provided for them and used their family in His big rescue plan.

## Bible Anchor

### Ruth 1-4

The book of Ruth begins during a hard time in Israel’s history. A famine came to Bethlehem. A famine is a time when food is very scarce. A man named Elimelech took his wife Naomi and their two sons to the land of Moab so they could find food.

Naomi’s story became very sad. Her husband died. Later, her two sons died too. Naomi was left with her two daughters-in-law, Orpah and Ruth. A daughter-in-law is a woman married to someone’s son. Naomi heard that the Lord had helped His people in Bethlehem by giving them food again, so she started home.

Naomi told Orpah and Ruth to return to their own families. Orpah cried and went back. Ruth chose to stay with Naomi. Ruth left her homeland and went with Naomi to Bethlehem. Ruth’s loyal love was not just a warm feeling. It was a costly choice to stay, serve, and trust Naomi’s God.

In Bethlehem, Ruth worked hard gathering grain in a field so she and Naomi could eat. In God’s providence, Ruth came to the field of Boaz, a kind and honorable man from Naomi’s family. Boaz protected Ruth, provided for her, and treated her with respect.

Boaz later became Ruth’s redeemer. In this story, a redeemer was a family protector who could rescue a relative from loss by buying back land and caring for the family line. Boaz married Ruth. They had a son named Obed. Obed became the grandfather of King David.

Ruth’s story shows God’s providence. Providence means God’s wise care and rule, even when people cannot see the whole plan yet. Ruth and Naomi could not see everything God was doing. But God was caring for them, and He was weaving their family into the bigger story that would one day lead to Jesus, the true and greater Redeemer.

## Story Trail

### 1. Naomi faced deep sadness

Naomi’s family left Bethlehem because of famine. Bethlehem means “house of bread,” but there was not enough food there at that time. Naomi, her husband, and their sons went to Moab.

Then Naomi’s husband died. Later her sons died too. This was deep grief. Grief is the sadness we feel when someone dies or when something precious is lost.

The Bible does not rush past Naomi’s sorrow. It lets us see that Naomi was hurting. She felt empty, and she did not pretend everything was fine.

**Teaching point:** God’s Word is honest about sadness. God sees His people when they grieve.

### 2. Ruth chose loyal love

Naomi told Ruth and Orpah to go back to their own families. Naomi thought she had nothing left to give them. Orpah cried and returned home, but Ruth clung to Naomi and would not leave her.

Ruth’s choice was loyal love. Loyal love means faithful kindness that stays, helps, and keeps a promise even when it is costly. Ruth was leaving her country, familiar places, and old life. She chose Naomi’s people and the Lord, Naomi’s God.

Ruth did not know exactly how everything would turn out. She took the next faithful step.

**Teaching point:** Faithful love is more than words. It shows up in choices, especially when love is hard.

### 3. Ruth and Naomi returned to Bethlehem

Ruth and Naomi arrived in Bethlehem at the beginning of the barley harvest. Harvest is the season when farmers gather food from the fields.

Naomi was still grieving. She said her life felt bitter. Bitter can mean angry, painful, or full of sorrow. Naomi’s words show how deeply she hurt. But even while Naomi felt empty, God was not done caring for her.

The timing mattered. Ruth and Naomi came when food was being gathered. God was already opening a way for provision. Provision means the help and supply people need.

**Teaching point:** We may not see the whole road, but God can be working before we understand what He is doing.

### 4. Ruth worked in the field

Ruth asked Naomi if she could go into the fields and gather leftover grain. God’s law had taught Israel to leave some grain for poor people, widows, and foreigners to gather. A widow is a woman whose husband has died. A foreigner is someone from another people or country.

Ruth was both a widow and a foreigner in Bethlehem. She worked humbly and diligently. Diligent means careful and hardworking.

Ruth “happened” to come to the field of Boaz, but the story helps us see more than luck. In God’s providence, Ruth came to the field of Boaz. God was guiding and providing. Boaz noticed Ruth’s faithful care for Naomi and treated Ruth with kindness and protection.

**Teaching point:** God often provides through ordinary things: work, kindness, wise laws, generous people, and open doors.

### 5. Boaz showed honorable kindness

Boaz was from Naomi’s family. He was wealthy enough to help and honorable enough to do what was right. He told Ruth to stay in his fields, gave her food, and made sure she was safe.

Boaz did not treat Ruth as unimportant because she was poor or from Moab. He saw her faithful love for Naomi and blessed her in the name of the Lord.

Honor means treating someone with respect because they matter. Boaz honored Ruth. Ruth also honored Boaz and Naomi. This story is full of people making faithful choices in ordinary moments.

**Teaching point:** God’s people should treat vulnerable people with protection, respect, and generosity.

### 6. Boaz became the redeemer

Naomi explained that Boaz was a close family member who could act as a redeemer. In Ruth’s time, this meant he could help rescue the family from loss by buying land connected to Naomi’s family and by marrying Ruth to continue the family line. A family line means the children, grandchildren, and later family who come after someone.

This part of the story may sound unfamiliar to us because their customs were different from ours. Customs are the normal practices people follow in a certain time and place. The important point is clear: Boaz used his strength and resources to protect and provide, not to take advantage.

Boaz handled the matter publicly and honestly at the city gate, the place where important community business was done. He did not sneak, force, or trick. He did what was right.

**Teaching point:** In this story, a faithful redeemer protects, provides, and acts with integrity.

### 7. God gave Ruth and Naomi a new family joy

Boaz married Ruth, and God gave them a son named Obed. Naomi, who had felt empty, now held a child connected to her family line. The women of Bethlehem rejoiced with Naomi because God had not forgotten her.

Obed became the father of Jesse, and Jesse became the father of King David. That means Ruth, a woman from Moab who trusted the Lord, became part of David’s family line.

Later, Jesus was born from David’s family line. Ruth’s story does not mean Ruth or Boaz could see every detail of the future. But readers can look back and see God’s bigger plan. God was caring for one grieving family, and He was also moving His rescue story forward.

**Teaching point:** God’s providence can turn ordinary faithfulness into part of a much bigger story.

## Hard Words Defined

- **Famine:** A time when food is very scarce.
- **Daughter-in-law:** A woman married to someone’s son.
- **Grief:** Deep sadness when someone dies or when something precious is lost.
- **Loyal love:** Faithful kindness that stays, helps, and keeps a promise even when it is costly.
- **Harvest:** The season when farmers gather food from the fields.
- **Bitter:** Painful, angry, or full of sorrow.
- **Provision:** The help and supply people need.
- **Widow:** A woman whose husband has died.
- **Foreigner:** Someone from another people or country.
- **Diligent:** Careful and hardworking.
- **Honor:** Treating someone with respect because they matter.
- **Redeemer:** A rescuer or family protector who pays a cost to bring someone out of loss or danger.
- **Customs:** Normal practices people follow in a certain time and place.
- **Integrity:** Doing what is right with honesty, even when it is complicated.
- **Providence:** God’s wise care and rule, even when people cannot see the whole plan yet.

## Honest Question

If God was caring for Naomi, why did such sad things happen to her family?

## Kid-Friendly Answer

The Bible does not tell us every reason why Naomi suffered. We should not pretend we know more than God tells us. Naomi’s sorrow was real, and God’s care was real too.

Ruth shows us that God can be working even when life feels empty. God did not shame Naomi for grieving. He cared for her through Ruth’s loyal love, Boaz’s kindness, food in Bethlehem, and a new family joy. God’s providence does not mean sadness is fake or easy. It means God is wise, kind, and able to keep caring for His people through the hardest parts of the story.

## Interactive Activity

### The Loyal Love Chain

**Supplies:**
- Four strips of paper
- Marker or crayons
- Tape or glue

**Set up:** Write one word on each strip:
1. “Stay”
2. “Serve”
3. “Protect”
4. “Provide”

**Steps:**
1. Read the four words aloud.
2. Ask: “How did Ruth show loyal love?” Let the child connect Ruth with “Stay” and “Serve.”
3. Ask: “How did Boaz show kindness?” Let the child connect Boaz with “Protect” and “Provide.”
4. Loop the strips into a paper chain. Say: “God used faithful choices to care for Ruth and Naomi.”
5. Add a final spoken link: “God’s bigger rescue story.” Explain that Ruth and Boaz became part of King David’s family line, and Jesus later came from David’s family.
6. Invite the child to use the chain to retell the story in four words.

**Teaching point:** Loyal love is not only a feeling. It is faithful action that God can use.

**Closing question:** What is one simple way you can show faithful kindness to someone in your family this week?

**Optional family challenge:** Choose one “loyal love” action today: help without being asked, stay near someone who is sad, share food or supplies, or speak respectfully about someone who feels left out.

## Parent/Teacher Talk

Ruth is a beautiful story, but it should be taught with both warmth and care. It is not mainly a romance lesson for children. It is a story about covenant kindness, meaning faithful love that keeps caring because of committed relationship, along with family care, honest work, protection for vulnerable people, and God’s providence.

Keep the tone gentle around Naomi’s losses. Children do not need graphic details. They do need to know that grief is real and that God sees grieving people. Also avoid making Ruth’s faithfulness sound like she earned God’s love. Ruth’s loyal love is good and beautiful, but God is the gracious provider and Redeemer behind the story.

### Conversation Questions

1. Why did Naomi’s family leave Bethlehem at the beginning of the story?
2. What sad losses did Naomi experience?
3. What choice did Ruth make when Naomi told her to go back?
4. How did Ruth work to help Naomi in Bethlehem?
5. How did Boaz protect and provide for Ruth?
6. What does “redeemer” mean in this story?
7. How did Ruth and Boaz become part of God’s bigger rescue story?
8. What is one way our family can show loyal love this week?

### Two mistakes to avoid

- **Mistake 1: Treating Ruth as only a love story.**  
  Better: “Ruth is about loyal love, providence, family protection, and God’s rescue plan. Boaz and Ruth’s marriage matters, but it is part of a bigger story.”

- **Mistake 2: Saying hard things happened because Naomi did something wrong.**  
  Better: “The book does not give us that explanation. We should not add it. We can say Naomi suffered deeply, and God cared for her faithfully.”

### If a child asks, “Was Ruth scared to move to Bethlehem?”

Answer gently: The Bible does not tell us exactly how Ruth felt. Moving to a new land after so much sadness may have been hard. What the Bible does show is that Ruth loved Naomi faithfully and chose to trust Naomi’s God.

### If a child asks, “What is a redeemer?”

Say: A redeemer is someone who rescues or buys back. In Ruth’s story, Boaz was a family redeemer who protected Ruth and Naomi and helped restore the family. The Bible’s bigger story points us to Jesus, the true and greater Redeemer, who rescues His people from sin and brings them to God.

### If a child is grieving

Do not rush the child to “feel better.” Ruth lets us say, “Naomi was very sad, and God still cared for her.” Offer a simple prayer, a quiet presence, and reassurance that sadness is not something we have to hide from God.
