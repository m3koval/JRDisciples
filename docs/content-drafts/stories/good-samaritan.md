## Big Question

Who is my neighbor, and what does love look like when someone needs help?

## Big Truth

Jesus teaches that loving our neighbor is not only knowing the right answer. Neighbor-love is mercy in action. Mercy means kind help given to someone in need, even when helping costs us time, comfort, or resources.

**Child recap:** A hurt man needed help. Two religious men passed by. A Samaritan stopped, cared for him, and showed mercy. Jesus teaches us to love our neighbors with real action.

## Bible Anchor

### Luke 10:25-37

A lawyer, a person trained in God’s law, stood up to test Jesus. He asked what he should do to inherit eternal life. Jesus pointed him back to what was written in the Law. The man answered that God’s people must love the Lord their God with all their heart, soul, strength, and mind, and love their neighbor as themselves.

Jesus said his answer was right. But the man wanted to justify himself. Justify means to try to prove that you are right or acceptable. So he asked Jesus who counted as his neighbor.

Jesus was not teaching that sinners can earn eternal life by doing enough kind actions. He was exposing the man’s heart and showing what real love looks like. Good works do not buy life with God, but God’s people are called to love with mercy.

Jesus answered with a parable. A parable is a teaching story Jesus used to reveal truth. In the story, a man was traveling from Jerusalem down to Jericho. Robbers attacked him, took what he had, hurt him badly, and left him nearly dead.

A priest came down the road. A priest was a religious leader who served in worship at the temple. He saw the hurt man, but he passed by on the other side. Then a Levite came. A Levite was from a tribe set apart for temple service. He also saw the hurt man and passed by on the other side.

Then a Samaritan came near. In Jesus’ day, many Jews and Samaritans did not trust or like each other. That means the listeners may have expected the Samaritan to be the last person who would help. But the Samaritan saw the hurt man and had compassion. Compassion means seeing someone’s pain and caring enough to help.

The Samaritan went to the man, cared for his wounds, put him on his own animal, brought him to an inn, and paid for his care. An inn was a place where travelers could stay. The Samaritan did not just feel sad. He showed mercy with action.

Jesus asked which person had been a neighbor to the man who was hurt. The answer was the one who showed mercy. Jesus told the man to go and do likewise.

## Story Trail

### 1. A man asked Jesus a serious question

A lawyer asked Jesus what he should do to inherit eternal life. This was a serious question, but Luke tells us the man was testing Jesus. Testing here means he was not simply asking with a humble heart; he was trying to challenge Jesus.

Jesus did not get trapped. He asked what was written in the Law. The man answered with two great commands: love God fully and love your neighbor as yourself.

Those are not tiny commands. Loving God means God is first in our worship, trust, thoughts, choices, and strength. Loving our neighbor means we care about another person’s good, not only our own comfort.

**Teaching point:** Knowing God’s commands matters, but God also calls us to live them.

### 2. The man tried to limit the command

The man wanted to justify himself, so he asked who counted as his neighbor.

His question may sound innocent, but it was a way to ask, “How small can I make this command? Who counts, and who does not?” He wanted a line around love that would make him look obedient.

Jesus did not give him a small list of people to love. Jesus told a story that showed neighbor-love is bigger and more costly than the man expected.

**Teaching point:** We should not use clever questions to shrink obedience. God’s love moves us toward mercy.

### 3. A traveler was hurt and left alone

In Jesus’ parable, a man traveled on the road from Jerusalem to Jericho. Robbers attacked him, stole from him, hurt him, and left him in danger.

This part is serious, but children do not need frightening details. The important point is that the man was helpless. He could not fix the problem by himself. He needed someone to stop and help.

A need is something important a person lacks. The hurt man needed safety, care, and rescue. He needed more than kind thoughts from far away.

**Teaching point:** Real mercy notices people who are in trouble and does not look away.

### 4. Two religious men saw him and passed by

A priest came down the road and saw the hurt man, but he passed by on the other side. Then a Levite came and did the same.

The priest and Levite knew many things about God’s law. They had religious roles that sounded important. But in Jesus’ story, knowing religious things did not lead them to mercy.

Jesus does not give every reason they passed by. Maybe they were busy. Maybe they were afraid. Maybe helping felt inconvenient or costly. The parable does not tell us to guess excuses for them. It shows that they saw a person in need and chose not to help.

**Teaching point:** It is possible to know Bible words and still miss love. God calls His people to mercy, not empty religion.

### 5. A Samaritan stopped and showed compassion

Then a Samaritan came near. This surprised Jesus’ listeners because many Jews and Samaritans treated each other like enemies. They had a long history of disagreement, suspicion, and unkindness.

But the Samaritan saw the hurt man and had compassion. Compassion was not just a feeling inside him. It moved his feet, hands, time, and money.

He went to the hurt man. He cared for his wounds. He put the man on his own animal, brought him to an inn, and made sure he would keep receiving care.

**Teaching point:** Compassion becomes mercy when it moves into helpful action.

### 6. Jesus showed what a neighbor does

At the end of the parable, Jesus asked which person proved to be a neighbor to the hurt man. The answer was not “the person with the right group label” or “the person with the most religious job.” The answer was the one who showed mercy.

Jesus turned the question around. The man had asked, “Who is my neighbor?” Jesus showed him a better question: “How can I be a neighbor who shows mercy?”

Neighbor-love does not mean a child must solve every problem alone. Children should not walk into unsafe situations without trusted adult help. But every child can learn the shape of mercy: notice, care, ask for help, and do what is wise and loving.

**Teaching point:** A faithful neighbor shows mercy with wisdom, courage, and action.

## Hard Words Defined

- **Lawyer:** In this story, a person trained in God’s law.
- **Inherit:** To receive something given to you, often as part of a family promise.
- **Eternal life:** Life with God forever, given by God and centered on knowing Him.
- **Justify:** To try to prove that you are right or acceptable.
- **Parable:** A teaching story Jesus used to reveal truth.
- **Priest:** A religious leader who served in worship at the temple.
- **Levite:** A person from the tribe of Levi, connected with temple service.
- **Samaritan:** A person from Samaria; in Jesus’ day many Jews and Samaritans had deep conflict with each other.
- **Compassion:** Seeing someone’s pain and caring enough to help.
- **Mercy:** Kind help given to someone in need, especially when helping costs us something.
- **Inn:** A place where travelers could stay.
- **Need:** Something important a person lacks, such as safety, food, care, or help.
- **Neighbor-love:** Love that treats another person as someone made by God and acts for that person’s good.

## Honest Question

Does Jesus mean I have to help every person by myself, even if it feels unsafe?

## Kid-Friendly Answer

No. Jesus teaches us not to ignore people in need, but He does not tell children to be reckless. Reckless means rushing into danger without wisdom.

If someone is hurt, bullied, lost, or in trouble, a child can show mercy by getting a trusted adult, praying, speaking kindly, staying nearby when it is safe, sharing appropriate supplies, or calling for help through an adult. Mercy is not pretending to be a superhero. Mercy is loving action with wisdom.

The Good Samaritan used what he had to help: his time, care, animal, money, and plan for continued care. We can ask, “What wise help can I give, and who should I ask to help with me?”

## Interactive Activity

### The Mercy Map

**Supplies:**
- One sheet of paper
- Marker or crayons
- Four small sticky notes or paper slips

**Set up:** Draw a simple road across the paper. Place a small mark in the middle for “someone needs help.” On the four sticky notes, write:
1. “Notice”
2. “Care”
3. “Get wise help”
4. “Act with mercy”

**Steps:**
1. Put “Notice” near the road. Ask: “What did the priest, Levite, and Samaritan all do first?” Answer: They saw the hurt man.
2. Put “Care” beside it. Ask: “Who cared enough to stop?” Answer: The Samaritan.
3. Put “Get wise help” near the inn. Explain that the Samaritan brought the man to a place where more care could continue.
4. Put “Act with mercy” at the end of the road. Name the Samaritan’s actions: he went near, cared for wounds, carried the man, brought him to the inn, and paid for help.
5. Invite the child to name one safe mercy action for today: include someone, tell an adult about a need, share supplies, comfort a sibling, or pray and then help.

**Teaching point:** Mercy follows a wise path: notice, care, get wise help, and act.

**Closing question:** Who is one person near you who may need kindness, help, or protection this week?

**Optional family challenge:** Choose one “neighbor-love” action together: bring a meal or note to someone, help a younger child, invite someone left out, donate needed supplies, or encourage a tired family member.

## Parent/Teacher Talk

The Good Samaritan is familiar, but it should not be flattened into “be nice.” Jesus told this parable after a man tried to justify himself and limit the command to love his neighbor. The story exposes empty religion, challenges prejudice, and shows mercy as concrete action.

For children, keep the road danger child-safe and avoid graphic violence. Emphasize that the traveler was in serious need, not that children should imagine frightening details. Also make safety clear: children can show mercy by involving trusted adults and choosing wise help.

### Conversation Questions

1. What question did the lawyer ask Jesus at the beginning?
2. What two loves did the lawyer name from God’s law?
3. Why did the man ask who counted as his neighbor?
4. What happened to the traveler on the road?
5. What did the priest and Levite do when they saw him?
6. Why was it surprising that a Samaritan helped?
7. What actions did the Samaritan take to show mercy?
8. What is one wise way our family can show neighbor-love this week?

### Two mistakes to avoid

- **Mistake 1: Teaching the story as only “be a nice person.”**
  Better: “Jesus teaches mercy that acts. Neighbor-love is costly, wise, and connected to loving God.”

- **Mistake 2: Making children feel responsible to fix every need alone.**
  Better: “Children can show mercy by noticing needs, caring, and getting trusted adult help. Wise mercy does not mean unsafe independence.”

### If a child asks, “Why did the priest and Levite pass by?”

Answer carefully: Jesus does not tell us exactly why they passed by. We should not invent a reason and act like the Bible says it. What Jesus makes clear is that they saw the man and did not help. The story warns us not to hide behind religious words while ignoring mercy.

### If a child asks, “Were Samaritans bad?”

Say: Samaritans were people from Samaria. In Jesus’ day, many Jews and Samaritans had a long conflict and often treated each other badly. Jesus surprised His listeners by making the Samaritan the person who showed mercy. Jesus was not saying one group is always good and another group is always bad. He was teaching that neighbor-love crosses the lines where people are tempted to avoid each other.

### If a child sees someone in trouble

Teach a simple safety line: “Do not ignore. Do not rush in alone. Get wise help.” If someone is hurt, unsafe, bullied, or lost, tell a trusted adult right away. If it is safe, stay kind and nearby. Pray, speak gently, and help in ways that are appropriate for a child.

## Prayer

Help us notice people in need, care with compassion, ask for wise help, and show mercy as faithful neighbors. Make our love for others flow from our love for You. In Jesus’ name, amen.
