## Big Question

Can we keep praying and doing what is right when people pressure us to stop?

## Big Truth

God is faithful, so His people can pray, tell the truth, and do what is right even when it is hard. God is able to deliver His people, and He is worthy of our trust even before we know how the story will end.

**Child recap:** Daniel kept praying to God. God protected Daniel in the lions’ den. We can trust God and do what is right under pressure.

## Bible Anchor

### Daniel 6

Daniel was one of the leaders in a powerful kingdom. He served with excellent work, honesty, and faithfulness. Some jealous officials wanted to trap him, but they could not find anything wrong with Daniel’s work. So they made a plan around the one thing they knew Daniel would not give up: his faithfulness to God.

The officials convinced King Darius to make a strong law for thirty days. During that time, no one was allowed to ask anything from any god or person except the king. Anyone who broke the law would be thrown into a den of lions.

Daniel heard about the law. He did not make a show to impress people, and he did not hide from God. He went home and prayed to God as he had done before. His enemies saw him praying and accused him before the king.

The king was upset because he cared about Daniel, but the law could not be changed. Daniel was put into the lions’ den, and a stone was placed over the opening. Early the next morning, the king hurried to the den. God had sent His angel and shut the lions’ mouths. Daniel was alive and unharmed.

Daniel’s rescue showed that God was powerful, faithful, and worthy of trust. Daniel did not obey God only when it was easy. He trusted God under pressure.

## Story Trail

### 1. Daniel was faithful before the test came

Daniel did not suddenly become faithful when danger arrived. Daniel 6 shows that he was already trustworthy in his work. The king planned to set Daniel over the whole kingdom because Daniel had an excellent spirit.

That made some other leaders jealous. Jealous means wanting what someone else has and feeling angry about it. They looked for a way to accuse Daniel, but they could not find corruption or carelessness in him. Corruption means using power in a dishonest or selfish way.

Daniel’s life was not perfect because only Jesus is sinless. But Daniel was known for integrity. Integrity means doing what is right, even when people are watching, and even when it is hard.

**Teaching point:** Faithfulness in small daily choices prepares us for bigger tests.

### 2. The jealous officials made a trap

The officials knew Daniel prayed to God. They did not find a work problem, so they tried to make Daniel’s worship of God look like a crime.

They persuaded King Darius to make a decree. A decree is an official law or command from a ruler. For thirty days, people could only make requests to the king. That law tried to push Daniel into a terrible choice: stop praying to God, or be punished.

This was pressure. Pressure is when people or circumstances push you toward a choice. Sometimes pressure can feel like a crowd saying, “Do what we want, not what is right.”

**Teaching point:** Not every rule from people is good. When human pressure tells us to disobey God, God comes first.

### 3. Daniel prayed as he had done before

Daniel knew about the decree. He also knew God was still God. Daniel went to his house and prayed toward Jerusalem, as he had done before.

The Bible does not say Daniel prayed to show off. It does not say he shouted in the street to start a fight. It says he kept praying as part of his faithful life with God.

Prayer is talking to God with trust. Daniel prayed when the law made prayer dangerous. Courage does not mean Daniel knew exactly what would happen next. Courage means he trusted God and did what was right.

**Teaching point:** Faithful courage is not loud pride. It is steady trust and obedience.

### 4. Daniel was accused and put in danger

The officials found Daniel praying and brought the accusation to the king. Accuse means to say someone has done wrong. In this case, Daniel really had broken the king’s decree, but he had not done anything wrong before God.

King Darius wanted to rescue Daniel, but he could not change the decree. Daniel was put into the lions’ den. This part of the story is serious, but we do not need scary details to understand it. Daniel was in real danger, and only God could deliver him.

Deliverance means rescue from danger or trouble. The king hoped Daniel’s God would deliver him, but Daniel had to spend the night in the den.

**Teaching point:** Trusting God does not always mean danger disappears immediately. It means God is with His people and is worthy of trust in the danger.

### 5. God shut the lions’ mouths

Early the next morning, the king hurried to the den and called for Daniel. Daniel answered. God had sent His angel and shut the lions’ mouths. Daniel was safe.

The Bible says no injury was found on Daniel because he had trusted in his God. God showed His power by protecting Daniel from the lions.

This does not mean faithful people will never suffer. Other Bible stories show that God’s people sometimes face pain, prison, or even death. But Daniel 6 teaches us that God is able to rescue, God sees His people, and no ruler is stronger than God.

**Teaching point:** God’s deliverance is powerful, and His faithfulness is greater than human threats.

### 6. The king honored Daniel’s God

After Daniel was rescued, King Darius sent a message through his kingdom telling people to honor Daniel’s God. He said Daniel’s God lives forever, His kingdom will not be destroyed, and He rescues and delivers.

Daniel’s faithfulness pointed beyond Daniel. It helped people see the living God.

When we pray, tell the truth, and do what is right, we are not trying to look impressive. We want our lives to point to God, who is faithful and good.

**Teaching point:** Integrity can become a witness. A witness is a person or action that points others toward what is true.

## Hard Words Defined

- **Faithfulness:** Continuing to trust and obey God, not only when it is easy.
- **Integrity:** Doing what is right with honesty, even when it is hard or when people are watching.
- **Jealous:** Feeling angry or unhappy because someone else has something you want.
- **Official:** A person who has a job of leadership or authority.
- **Corruption:** Dishonest or selfish use of power.
- **Decree:** An official law or command from a ruler.
- **Pressure:** A push toward a choice, especially when people want you to do what they say.
- **Accuse:** To say someone has done wrong.
- **Prayer:** Talking to God with trust.
- **Courage:** Doing what is right while trusting God, even when you feel afraid or do not know what will happen.
- **Deliverance:** Rescue from danger or trouble.
- **Witness:** Someone or something that points others toward what is true.

## Honest Question

If Daniel trusted God, why did God let him be thrown into the lions’ den at all?

## Kid-Friendly Answer

Trusting God does not mean hard things never happen. Daniel still faced danger. But God was not weak, absent, or surprised. God was with Daniel, and God delivered Daniel in a way that showed His power to the king and the kingdom.

Sometimes God rescues His people from danger right away. Sometimes He helps His people endure hard things. Either way, God is faithful. Daniel’s courage was not based on knowing every detail of the ending. Daniel trusted God because God is worthy of trust.

## Interactive Activity

### The Pressure Path

**Supplies:**
- Three small cards or paper slips
- Marker
- Optional: a small toy figure or game piece

**Set up:** Write one phrase on each card:
1. “Pray to God”
2. “Tell the truth”
3. “Do what is right”

Place the cards in a simple path across the table or floor.

**Steps:**
1. Read each card aloud and ask: “When might this be easy?”
2. Ask: “When might this be hard?” Examples: a friend laughs at prayer, someone wants you to lie, or a crowd wants you to join something wrong.
3. Move the toy figure to the first card. Say: “Daniel kept praying when pressure came.”
4. Move to the second card. Say: “Daniel’s enemies could not find dishonesty in his work.”
5. Move to the third card. Say: “Daniel trusted God and did what was right, even when he did not know what would happen.”
6. Invite the child to choose one card as a next step for today.

**Teaching point:** Courage grows when we practice faithful choices in ordinary moments.

**Closing question:** What is one place where you need God’s help to pray, tell the truth, or do what is right this week?

**Optional prayer habit challenge:** Pause and pray at three ordinary times today: morning, afternoon, and bedtime. Keep the prayers simple: “God, help me trust You and do what is right.”

## Parent/Teacher Talk

Daniel 6 is a strong story for teaching courage and integrity, but it needs careful framing. Do not teach it as, “If you obey God, nothing bad will happen.” Daniel did obey God, and he still went into the den. The better lesson is: God is faithful, God is able to rescue, and God is worthy of trust even when obedience is costly.

### Conversation Questions

1. Why were the other officials unable to find fault in Daniel’s work?
2. What did the new decree try to make Daniel stop doing?
3. How did Daniel respond when he knew the decree had been signed?
4. What is the difference between showing off and faithfully continuing to pray?
5. How did God deliver Daniel?
6. What is one situation where it may be hard for a child to tell the truth, pray, or do what is right?

### Two mistakes to avoid

- **Mistake 1: Making Daniel the hero as if he saved himself.**  
  Better: “Daniel was faithful, but God was the Deliverer. Daniel trusted God; God shut the lions’ mouths.”

- **Mistake 2: Promising children that obedience always leads to easy outcomes.**  
  Better: “God can rescue in amazing ways, and He is always faithful. Sometimes He rescues us from trouble, and sometimes He strengthens us through trouble.”

### If a child asks, “Was Daniel scared?”

Answer gently: The Bible does not tell us exactly what Daniel felt that night. He may have felt fear, and he may have felt peace. What the Bible does show is that Daniel trusted God and kept doing what was right.

### If a child asks, “Should I disobey rules like Daniel did?”

Answer carefully: Most rules from parents, teachers, and leaders should be obeyed. Daniel disobeyed this decree because it commanded him to stop praying to God. If a rule tells you to sin or stop following God, talk to a trusted Christian adult and ask for wise help.

### If a child is afraid of the lions’ den

Keep the focus steady and child-safe. The lions were real and the danger was real, but the main point is not fear. The main point is that God protected Daniel and showed that He is greater than kings, laws, and lions.
