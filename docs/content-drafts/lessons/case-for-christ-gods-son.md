## Big Question

Is Jesus really God’s Son, and what does that mean?

## Big Truth

Jesus is the Son of God: He is truly God, truly became human, and perfectly shows us the Father. “Son of God” does not mean Jesus was created by God or became God later. It means Jesus has a one-of-a-kind relationship with the Father and shares God’s own glory, life, and authority.

Christians believe this because Jesus revealed it, His disciples confessed it, and John wrote his Gospel as testimony about what Jesus did and said so people would believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and have life in His name.

**Child recap:** Jesus is God the Son. He was never created. He became human to save us.

## Bible Anchor

**Matthew 16:13-17 (WEB)**

> Now when Jesus came into the parts of Caesarea Philippi, he asked his disciples, saying,
> “Who do men say that I, the Son of Man, am?”
> They said, “Some say John the Baptizer, some, Elijah, and others, Jeremiah, or one of the prophets.”
> He said to them,
> “But who do you say that I am?”
> Simon Peter answered, “You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.”
> Jesus answered him,
> “Blessed are you, Simon Bar Jonah, for flesh and blood has not revealed this to you, but my Father who is in heaven.

Peter did not say Jesus was only a brave teacher or a good example. He confessed that Jesus is the Christ and the Son of the living God. Jesus said Peter did not figure this out by human guessing. The Father revealed the truth.

**John 1:1-5, 14 (WEB)**

> In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.
> The same was in the beginning with God.
> All things were made through him. Without him was not anything made that has been made.
> In him was life, and the life was the light of men.
> The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness hasn’t overcome it.
> The Word became flesh, and lived among us. We saw his glory, such glory as of the one and only Son of the Father, full of grace and truth.

John calls Jesus “the Word.” He says the Word was with God and the Word was God. Then John says the Word became flesh — Jesus really became human and lived among people. Jesus is not less than God, and He is not pretending to be human. He is truly God the Son who truly came near.

**John 20:31 (WEB)**

> but these are written, that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that believing you may have life in his name.

John tells us why he wrote: so we may believe in Jesus and have life in His name.

## Evidence / Teaching Trail

### 1. Jesus asked the most important question

Jesus asked His disciples, “Who do you say that I am?” That question still matters. Some people in Jesus’ day thought He was a prophet. A prophet is someone God sends to speak His message. Jesus is greater than a prophet. He is the Christ, the promised King, and the Son of the living God.

**Child-clear picture:** If someone asks, “Who is that person?” it is not enough to say, “He seems nice.” If the person is the king, his identity matters. Jesus’ identity matters most of all because trusting the wrong idea about Jesus leads us away from the truth.

### 2. “Son of God” does not mean Jesus was created

When kids hear “son,” they may think, “A son is younger than his dad, so maybe Jesus started later than the Father.” That is not what the Bible means. John says, “In the beginning was the Word.” Before anything was made, the Son already was. All things were made through Him.

Jesus is the eternal Son. Eternal means He has no beginning and no end. He did not become God. He did not get promoted into God’s family. The Son has always been with the Father and has always been God.

This is a deep truth. We do not have to pretend it is tiny. We can say it carefully: There is one God. The Father is God. The Son is God. The Holy Spirit is God. The Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are distinct persons, not three gods. Christians call this the Trinity.

### 3. Jesus truly became human

John says, “The Word became flesh, and lived among us.” Flesh means real human life. Jesus was not God wearing a costume. He was born, grew up, got tired, ate food, cried, obeyed the Father, loved people, and suffered on the cross.

This matters because Jesus came close enough to save us. Only God can rescue sinners, and only a real human could stand in the place of humans. Jesus, the Son of God, is the perfect Savior.

### 4. Jesus shows us what God is like

John says Jesus came “full of grace and truth.” Grace means kind favor we did not earn. Truth means what is real and right. Jesus did not show grace by ignoring sin, and He did not show truth by being cold. He brought both together perfectly.

When Jesus welcomed children, healed the hurting, corrected proud hearts, forgave sinners, and obeyed the Father, He showed the heart of God. We do not have to guess whether God is holy, loving, truthful, and merciful. We look at Jesus.

### 5. Believing Jesus is God’s Son calls for trust, not just facts

John did not write only so readers could pass a Bible quiz. He wrote so people would believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and have life in His name. Believing means trusting Jesus, not merely knowing a sentence about Him.

A child may say, “I believe this chair can hold me,” but trust is shown when the child sits in the chair. In a greater way, believing in Jesus means resting your hope in Him: listening to His Word, turning from sin, asking Him for mercy, and following Him as Lord.

## Hard Words Defined

- **Christ:** The promised King and Savior God sent. “Christ” means “Anointed One.”
- **Son of God:** Jesus’ special title showing His one-of-a-kind relationship with the Father, His divine identity, and His authority. It does not mean Jesus was created.
- **Eternal:** Having no beginning and no end.
- **The Word:** John’s name for Jesus in John 1. It shows that Jesus reveals God and was with God and was God from the beginning.
- **Flesh:** Real human life. Jesus became truly human, not pretend human.
- **Glory:** The shining greatness, honor, and beauty of who God is.
- **Grace:** God’s kind favor that we do not earn.
- **Truth:** What is real, right, and faithful.
- **Trinity:** The Christian teaching that there is one God in three persons: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.
- **Faith:** Trusting God because He is true and trustworthy.

## Honest Question

If Jesus is God’s Son, does that mean Christians believe in more than one God?

## Kid-Friendly Answer

No. Christians believe there is one God. The Bible teaches that the Father is God, the Son is God, and the Holy Spirit is God. They are not three separate gods, and they are not one person wearing three masks. God is one God in three persons.

That is bigger than our brains, but it is not nonsense. Some true things are bigger than us. A little cup cannot hold the whole ocean, but it can hold true ocean water. Our minds cannot hold everything about God, but we can truly know what God has told us.

So when we say Jesus is God’s Son, we are not saying He is a smaller god or a created helper. We are saying what Scripture teaches: Jesus is the eternal Son, truly God, who became truly human to save us.

## Interactive Activity

### Identity Cards: Who Does the Bible Say Jesus Is?

**Supplies:**
- Five index cards or paper slips
- Marker
- Bible or printed passage references

**Set up:** Write one phrase on each card:
1. Prophet
2. Christ
3. Son of the living God
4. The Word
5. Savior

**Steps:**
1. Read or carefully summarize Matthew 16:13-17.
2. Let children hold up the card “Prophet” when they hear what some people thought about Jesus.
3. Let children hold up “Christ” and “Son of the living God” when Peter answers Jesus.
4. Read or carefully summarize John 1:1-5 and 1:14. Hold up “The Word.” Ask, “Was the Word created, or was the Word already there in the beginning?”
5. Read John 20:31. Hold up “Savior” and ask, “What does John want us to do with the truth about Jesus?”

**Teaching point:** The Bible gives us true names and titles for Jesus so we do not have to guess who He is.

**Recap question:** What mistake should we never make when we hear “Son of God”? Answer: We should never think Jesus was created or became God later. Jesus is the eternal Son.

**Optional older-kid challenge:** Sort the cards into two groups: “What people guessed” and “What God reveals.” Talk about why Peter needed God to reveal the truth.

## Parent/Teacher Talk

This lesson teaches a high doctrine in child-clear language. Do not flatten it into “Jesus is God’s child like we are God’s children.” Believers are adopted as God’s children by grace. Jesus is the eternal Son by nature. He is unique.

### Conversation Questions

1. What question did Jesus ask His disciples in Matthew 16?
2. What did Peter say about Jesus?
3. Why does John 1:1 matter for understanding who Jesus is?
4. What does “the Word became flesh” mean?
5. What is one way trusting Jesus is different from only knowing facts about Jesus?

### Two mistakes to avoid

- **Mistake 1: Making “Son of God” sound like Jesus was created.**  
  Better: “Jesus is the eternal Son. He has always been with the Father and has always been God.”

- **Mistake 2: Explaining the Trinity with broken object lessons.**  
  Avoid saying God is like water changing forms or one person wearing different hats. Those pictures can teach the wrong idea. Better: “There is one God in three persons. This is deep, but God has truly told us who He is.”

### If a child asks, “Is Jesus the same person as the Father?”

Answer gently: No. The Son is God, and the Father is God, but the Son is not the Father. The Father sent the Son, the Son became human to save us, and the Holy Spirit helps us trust and follow Jesus. There is one God in three persons.

### If a child asks, “Can I understand everything about the Trinity?”

Answer gently: No creature can understand everything about God. But God has told us true things. We can trust what He says, worship Him humbly, and keep learning from Scripture.
