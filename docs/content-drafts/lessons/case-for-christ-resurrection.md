## Big Question

Did Jesus really rise from the dead, or did His friends only wish it was true?

## Big Truth

Christians believe Jesus rose bodily from the dead because God’s Word says it happened, Jesus’ followers saw Him alive, and the earliest Christian message was built on His death, burial, resurrection, and appearances.

That does not mean every question is silly. Thomas had a serious question. Paul named real witnesses. The Bible does not tell kids to pretend. It invites us to look carefully, listen humbly, and trust the risen Jesus.

## Bible Anchor

**1 Corinthians 15:3-8 (WEB)**

> For I delivered to you first of all that which I also received: that Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures,
> that he was buried, that he was raised on the third day according to the Scriptures,
> and that he appeared to Cephas, then to the twelve.
> Then he appeared to over five hundred brothers at once, most of whom remain until now, but some have also fallen asleep.
> Then he appeared to James, then to all the apostles,
> and last of all, as to the child born at the wrong time, he appeared to me also.

Paul says the good news he preached included four important facts: Jesus died for our sins, was buried, was raised, and appeared to witnesses. He even mentions that many witnesses were still alive when he wrote, which means people could ask about what they had seen.

**John 20:24-31 (WEB)**

> But Thomas, one of the twelve, called Didymus, wasn’t with them when Jesus came.
> The other disciples therefore said to him, “We have seen the Lord!”
> But he said to them, “Unless I see in his hands the print of the nails, put my finger into the print of the nails, and put my hand into his side, I will not believe.”
> After eight days again his disciples were inside, and Thomas was with them. Jesus came, the doors being locked, and stood in the middle, and said,
> “Peace be to you.”
> Then he said to Thomas,
> “Reach here your finger, and see my hands. Reach here your hand, and put it into my side. Don’t be unbelieving, but believing.”
> Thomas answered him, “My Lord and my God!”
> Jesus said to him,
> “Because you have seen me,
> you have believed. Blessed are those who have not seen, and have believed.”
> Therefore Jesus did many other signs in the presence of his disciples, which are not written in this book;
> but these are written, that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that believing you may have life in his name.

John shows us that Jesus was not angry at Thomas for needing help. Jesus gave Thomas a real answer and called him to believe.

## Evidence / Teaching Trail

### 1. The resurrection was part of the first Christian message

Paul says he delivered what he had received: Christ died, was buried, was raised, and appeared to people. This was not a tiny side idea added later. From the beginning, Christians preached that Jesus rose from the dead.

**Child-clear picture:** If your whole soccer team keeps telling the same main report after the game — “We played, the score was 3-2, and Jordan kicked the winning goal” — that main report matters. Paul gives the main report of the gospel: Jesus died, was buried, rose, and appeared.

### 2. Jesus’ tomb was not the end of the story

The Bible says Jesus truly died and was buried. The resurrection does not mean Jesus merely “felt better” or that people only remembered Him fondly. Christians believe Jesus rose with a real body. He could be seen. He could speak. He still carried the marks of the cross.

This matters because Jesus did not only defeat sadness. He defeated sin and death. The resurrection shows that God accepted Jesus’ sacrifice and that Jesus is Lord.

### 3. The witnesses were specific people, not a foggy rumor

Paul names Cephas, also called Peter, the twelve, more than five hundred brothers, James, all the apostles, and himself. John tells us about Thomas. The Bible does not say, “Somebody somewhere maybe heard something.” It points to people who claimed they saw Jesus alive.

We should be honest: some people still reject the evidence. Evidence helps us think carefully, but evidence does not force every heart to trust God. Faith is trusting the true God, not pretending there are no questions.

### 4. Thomas shows us how Jesus handles honest questions

Thomas said he would not believe unless he saw Jesus’ wounds. Eight days later, Jesus came to him. Jesus did not make a joke about Thomas. Jesus invited him to see and believe. Thomas answered, “My Lord and my God!”

Kids need to know this: honest questions can be brought to Jesus. A proud question says, “I will only believe if I get to be in charge.” A humble question says, “Lord, help me understand what is true.”

### 5. The resurrection changes what we do next

If Jesus rose from the dead, then He is not just an interesting teacher from long ago. He is the living Lord. We can trust Him, listen to Him, confess sin to Him, follow Him, and tell others about Him with courage and kindness.

The right response is not to win arguments or look smarter than other people. The right response is worship, trust, repentance, and faithful witness.

## Hard Words Defined

- **Resurrection:** Coming back from death to life. Jesus’ resurrection means He rose bodily from the dead and will never die again.
- **Bodily:** With a real body, not only as an idea, dream, or memory.
- **Witness:** A person who saw something happen and can tell others about it.
- **Apostle:** A messenger Jesus specially sent. The apostles taught about Jesus with authority from Him.
- **Gospel:** The good news that Jesus died for our sins, rose again, and gives forgiveness and life to those who trust Him.
- **Evidence:** Clues, facts, and testimony that help us know whether something is true.
- **Faith:** Trusting God because He is true and trustworthy.
- **Repentance:** Turning away from sin and turning toward God.

## Honest Question

If I did not see Jesus rise from the dead with my own eyes, how can I believe it?

## Kid-Friendly Answer

You believe many true things you did not see yourself. You may believe where you were born because trusted people and records tell you. You may believe a country exists before you visit it because many witnesses, maps, photos, and reports point to it.

For Jesus’ resurrection, Christians trust God’s Word first. We also see that the Bible gives real reasons: Jesus really died, His followers said they saw Him alive, the message was preached early and publicly, and people like Thomas were invited to move from doubt to faith.

Believing in Jesus is not the same as guessing. It is trusting the risen Lord who has given us His Word and good reasons to believe.

## Interactive Activity

### Witness Web: What Did They Say?

**Supplies:**
- Paper or whiteboard
- Marker
- Sticky notes or small paper cards

**Set up:** Write “Jesus rose from the dead” in the center of the board or paper.

**Steps:**
1. Read 1 Corinthians 15:3-8 or summarize it carefully from the Bible.
2. Give each child a sticky note with one witness group from the passage: Peter/Cephas, the twelve, more than five hundred, James, all the apostles, Paul.
3. Let children place the notes around the center statement, making a “witness web.”
4. Read or summarize John 20:24-31 and add Thomas to the web.
5. Ask: “Why is it helpful that the Bible names witnesses instead of only saying, ‘A rumor happened’?”

**Teaching point:** The resurrection is not presented as a secret wish. The Bible gives testimony from real people who said they saw Jesus alive.

**Optional challenge for older kids:** Put these cards in order: died, buried, raised, appeared. Then explain why each part matters.

## Parent/Teacher Talk

Use this lesson to build confidence without creating pride. Children should not leave thinking, “People who doubt are dumb.” They should leave thinking, “Jesus is alive, truth matters, and I can bring honest questions into the light.”

### Conversation Questions

1. What four facts does Paul name in 1 Corinthians 15:3-4?
2. Why does it matter that Jesus was buried before He was raised?
3. Who are some witnesses Paul mentions?
4. How did Jesus respond to Thomas?
5. What is one faithful next step if we believe Jesus is risen?

### Two mistakes to avoid

- **Mistake 1: Overstating the evidence.**  
  Do not say, “Everyone who looks at the evidence must become a Christian.” Better: “God gives strong reasons to believe, and we ask Him for humble hearts to trust what is true.”

- **Mistake 2: Shaming questions.**  
  Do not say, “Good Christians never wonder about hard things.” Better: “Honest questions can become a path toward deeper trust when we bring them to God’s Word with humility.”

### If a child asks, “What if someone says Jesus only rose in people’s hearts?”

Answer gently: The Bible teaches more than warm memories. It says Jesus died, was buried, was raised, and appeared. Thomas saw the marks from the cross. Christians believe Jesus rose bodily — really alive, not only remembered.
