## QA Findings

### Biblical faithfulness and Scripture handling

- The draft accurately summarizes Daniel 6: Daniel’s excellent and honest service, the jealous officials’ trap, the thirty-day decree, Daniel’s continued prayer, the sealed lions’ den, God sending His angel to shut the lions’ mouths, and Darius honoring Daniel’s God.
- Scripture handling is safe. The draft uses citations and careful summaries rather than direct Bible quotations, so there are no invented or unverified ESV quote blockers.
- The theology is sound: Daniel is presented as faithful, but God remains the Deliverer. The draft does not imply Daniel saved himself.
- The draft avoids prosperity-style overpromising. It clearly teaches that obedience does not guarantee easy outcomes and that God is worthy of trust before we know how a situation will end.
- “The law could not be changed” is an acceptable child-level summary of the irrevocable law/decree of the Medes and Persians in Daniel 6.
- “Daniel’s life was not perfect because only Jesus is sinless” is a helpful Christian guardrail against over-idealizing Daniel.
- “The king honored Daniel’s God” is acceptable for a child-facing summary, though Daniel 6 uses stronger language about trembling and fearing before Daniel’s God.
- The draft wisely avoids graphic details about the later judgment on Daniel’s accusers.

### Child clarity, engagement, and family usefulness

- The tone is warm, truthful, and child-safe for ages 6–10, with especially good restraint around the lions’ den danger.
- Hard words are defined well, including faithfulness, integrity, decree, pressure, courage, deliverance, and witness.
- The child recap is helpful because the Big Truth is accurate but long.
- The “Pressure Path” activity is low-prep, participatory, and directly reinforces the lesson’s faithful next steps: pray, tell the truth, and do what is right.
- Parent/Teacher Talk is strong. The “Two mistakes to avoid” section gives adults clear guardrails against making Daniel the self-saving hero or promising children that obedience always prevents hardship.
- The answers to likely child questions are careful and useful, especially on fear, civil disobedience, and trusted-adult guidance.

## Non-blocking Improvement Notes

1. The Big Truth is strong but long for younger children. The existing child recap helps; promotion could keep both a full version and a shorter child-facing version.
2. The Story Trail is clear but somewhat dense for ages 6–7. During promotion, use shorter reading chunks or simplify repeated abstract vocabulary where possible.
3. The Hard Words list is helpful but long. Consider highlighting the most essential 5–6 terms in the child-facing route while retaining fuller support for adults if needed.
4. The definition of integrity could be sharpened later to include doing right even when no one is watching, not only when people are watching.
5. The child-facing line “Not every rule from people is good” is biblically appropriate in context, and the Parent/Teacher Talk frames it well. During promotion, keep the trusted-adult guidance close to any civil-disobedience application.
6. Promotion notes request preserving a “5-part story summary,” while the draft’s Story Trail has 6 parts. Reconcile that count during data promotion.
