# Visual Learning Pack Brief: case-for-christ-resurrection

Lane: lessons  
Title: Did Jesus Really Rise from the Dead?  
Anchors: 1 Corinthians 15:3-8; John 20:24-31  
Gate: PASS

## Scope

Create a visual learning pack for exactly this lesson. Do not generate images in this stage. Do not publish.

Lesson focus: help kids examine why Christians believe Jesus rose bodily from the dead without overstating evidence or shaming honest questions.

Visual tone: hopeful, reverent, curious, warm, historically honest, child-safe. The pack should support the lesson’s apologetics line: Scripture is primary; named witnesses and historical context give good reasons for confidence, but visuals must not claim archaeology “proves” the resurrection.

## Character Canon

Use polished 3D animated human children, not minifigs.

Recurring cast block for prompt consistency:

Michael is a 9–10-year-old polished 3D animated human boy with child proportions, curly brown hair, warm tan skin, a green adventure tunic/outfit, and a small explorer satchel or lantern; he is the steady leader, brave but humble. Rosie is an 8–9-year-old polished 3D animated human girl with child proportions, wavy brown hair, a purple adventure dress/tunic, and a small Bible book or Scripture-light object; she is gentle, thoughtful, and truth-centered. Joseph is an 8–9-year-old polished 3D animated human boy with child proportions, dark curly hair, a blue adventure outfit, and a small notebook or tool pouch; he is curious, energetic, kind, and teachable. Gracie is a 6–7-year-old polished 3D animated human girl with younger child proportions, warm brown skin, black curly hair in two puff ponytails or short curls, and a yellow rain hood/rain cloak in storm scenes; she is bright, trusting, and brave in a younger-child way.

Consistency rule: keep each child the same age, height relationship, face shape, hair, skin tone, and clothing colors across every image. Only include characters named for the scene. Toy-brick/voxel styling may apply to environments and props, not the children.

## Global Prompt Strategy

Preferred style phrase:

> high-quality children’s Bible adventure illustration, polished 3D animated human children, blocky toy-brick / voxel diorama environment, open glowing Bible as miniature landscape, warm dawn light, hopeful reverent atmosphere, child-safe apologetics adventure

Global negatives:

> no readable text, no fake Bible words, no logos, no watermarks, no gore, no horror, no graphic crucifixion, no bloody wounds, no sensational wound close-ups, no photorealistic suffering, no minifig children, no aging up or recasting characters, no shaming doubters, no archaeology-proves-resurrection claim

Render Bible verses, witness names, card labels, lesson titles, and captions in site UI/HTML, not inside generated images.

## Required Visual Slots

### 1. Hero Visual — “The Witness Trail”

Concept: Michael, Rosie, and Joseph stand on an open glowing Bible that becomes a miniature Jerusalem-area landscape. A warm evidence trail leads from a sealed/empty rock-cut tomb silhouette toward simple blank witness cards and dawn light.

Purpose: Introduce the big question with hope and careful investigation, not mystery-horror.

Prompt notes:
- Include Michael with lantern/satchel, Rosie with small Bible/Scripture-light object, Joseph with notebook/tool pouch.
- Use empty tomb imagery, warm sunrise, and a trail of light.
- Do not depict the risen Jesus directly.
- Do not include readable text on cards or Bible pages.

UI text/caption suggestion:
- “Christ died, was buried, was raised, and appeared to witnesses.”

### 2. Bible Truth / Story Truth Visual — “Died, Buried, Raised, Appeared”

Concept: Four symbolic stations across open Bible pages: a distant cross shadow, a closed tomb stone, dawn light at an empty tomb entrance, and gathered blank witness cards or small figures listening.

Purpose: Reinforce Paul’s gospel summary in 1 Corinthians 15:3-8 and Thomas’ movement from honest question to worship in John 20:24-31.

Prompt notes:
- Keep the four parts visually clear without generated words.
- Make the resurrection bodily and historical through an empty tomb and witnesses, not a ghostly memory scene.
- Avoid Jesus-as-light-only ambiguity if representing the risen Christ; safest route is no direct depiction of Jesus.

UI text/caption suggestion:
- “The Bible says Jesus really died, was buried, was raised, and appeared to real witnesses.”

### 3. Artifact / History Visual — “First-Century Tomb Reconstruction”

Concept: Original historically informed reconstruction of a first-century Jewish rock-cut tomb with entrance stone, burial bench/niche, simple cloth/spice props, and Jerusalem-area stone landscape.

Purpose: Explain burial customs and the setting behind “He was buried” without pretending we have a photograph of Jesus’ tomb.

Required label:
- “Original reconstruction based on first-century Jewish burial practices; not a photograph of Jesus’ tomb.”

Historical notes:
- Rock-cut tombs and kokhim/loculi niches are useful context.
- Rolling-stone tomb imagery is plausible for some elite Judean tombs but should not be presented as guaranteed for Jesus’ tomb.
- Possible supporting artifact panels later: ossuaries, Pilate Stone, Yehohanan crucifixion heel bone, early manuscript visuals such as P46/P52. Use these only as context, not as proof of resurrection.

License cautions:
- If using real photos, verify exact open license/public-domain status before production: CC BY, CC BY-SA, public domain, or explicit institutional open access.
- Do not use museum/tourism/Google-result images without reuse rights.
- Do not imply any photographed tomb is Jesus’ exact tomb unless a caption clearly says “traditional,” “possible,” or “illustrative.”

### 4. Activity / Challenge Visual — “Witness Web”

Concept: A whiteboard/paper tabletop or open Bible scene with blank sticky-note cards radiating from a central glow. Michael, Rosie, Joseph, and optionally Gracie arrange the cards with curiosity and teamwork.

Purpose: Support the lesson activity where kids place witness groups around “Jesus rose from the dead.”

Prompt notes:
- Cards must be blank in generated art.
- UI/HTML should add labels: Peter/Cephas, the Twelve, 500+, James, all apostles, Paul, Thomas.
- Keep expressions thoughtful and encouraged. Thomas must not be mocked or shown as foolish.

Challenge UI idea:
- “Put the cards in order: died, buried, raised, appeared. Then explain why each part matters.”

### 5. Thumbnail Visual — “Did Jesus Really Rise?”

Concept: High-readability thumbnail with dawn-lit empty tomb, open glowing Bible, magnifying glass/evidence badge, and Junior Disciples silhouettes or faces looking hopeful.

Purpose: Invite clicks with clarity, not sensationalism.

Prompt notes:
- Strong silhouette and warm light.
- No small generated text.
- Do not show Jesus’ face/body or wounds.
- UI may overlay the title separately.

## Optional Short Video Concepts

1. “Three Facts Paul Passed On” — 6–8 seconds. Camera glides over open Bible pages from cross shadow to tomb to dawn to witness cards. UI overlays: died, buried, raised, appeared.
2. “Thomas Asked Honestly” — 6–8 seconds. A respectful locked-room-inspired diorama: disciples gathered, Thomas thoughtful, warm light entering frame. No wound close-ups; no mocking. UI overlay explains Jesus answered Thomas graciously.
3. “Witness Web Comes Together” — 6 seconds. Blank cards gently connect with lines of light around an open Bible. UI labels added outside generated video.
4. “What History Can and Can’t Show” — 8 seconds. Tomb reconstruction, ancient map, manuscript/parchment motif. UI caption: “History gives context; God’s Word gives the message.”

## Open-License / Public-Domain Guidance

Real historical photos may be used only if the exact asset license is verified before production. Prefer:
- Wikimedia Commons files with CC BY, CC BY-SA, or public-domain status.
- Museum open-access collections with explicit reuse terms.
- Original reconstructions created for Junior Disciples.

Avoid:
- Unlicensed museum photos, tourism photos, Bible encyclopedia images, Google image results, and modern copyrighted artwork.
- Public-domain paintings of “Doubting Thomas” if they are too graphic, adult-coded, or focused on wound inspection; an original reverent reconstruction is safer.

All historical reconstructions must be clearly labeled as original/historically informed reconstructions, not direct evidence photographs.

## Risks to Avoid

- Overstating the evidence visually or implying artifacts prove the resurrection by themselves.
- Graphic Passion imagery: blood, nails, wounds, suffering close-ups.
- Showing Thomas as foolish, guilty, or mocked.
- Generated readable Bible text, witness names, titles, or fake inscriptions.
- Making Jesus appear ghostly in a way that undermines bodily resurrection.
- Recasting, aging, or changing the Junior Disciples children.
- Using real historical photos without verified reuse rights.
