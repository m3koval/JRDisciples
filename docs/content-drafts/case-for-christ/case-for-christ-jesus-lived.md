## Big Question

Did Jesus really live on earth, or is He only a story people made up later?

## Big Truth

Jesus really lived in real history. He was born in a real place, under real rulers, among real people, and even people who did not worship Him knew He existed.

Christians believe more than “Jesus existed.” We believe Jesus is the Son of God who became truly human to save us. But this lesson starts with a simple historical question: **Was Jesus a real person?** The answer is yes.

**History** means the study of real people and events from the past. **Sources** are records or witnesses that help us learn what happened.

## Evidence Trail

Here are some reasons we can say Jesus really lived:

1. **The Bible places Jesus in real history.**  
   Luke does not write like he is telling a pretend fairy tale. He names rulers, places, family lines, a census, Bethlehem, Nazareth, Jerusalem, and many witnesses. A **census** is an official counting of people, often connected to taxes or government records.

2. **The Gospels were written close to the events.**  
   Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John were not written hundreds of years after everyone forgot what happened. They were connected to the first generation of Christians, when people could still ask witnesses and check claims.

3. **Early Christians preached Jesus publicly.**  
   The first Christians did not say, “Believe a secret story from far away.” They preached in the same region where Jesus had lived, taught, died, and risen. If Jesus had never existed, people could have answered, “No one here has ever heard of that man.”

4. **Jesus’ enemies treated Him as real.**  
   In the New Testament, religious leaders and Roman authorities do not argue that Jesus was imaginary. They argue about His power, His claims, and what to do with Him. Even opposition can become a clue: enemies may disagree with Jesus, but they still knew He existed.

5. **Non-Christian ancient writers mention Jesus or early Christians.**  
   Some ancient writers outside the Bible mention Jesus, His execution, or the movement that followed Him. These writers were not trying to help Christianity. That makes their testimony useful as historical evidence. They show that Jesus was not invented by Christians centuries later.

Evidence does not replace faith. Evidence gives us good reasons to trust that our faith is not floating in make-believe. Jesus stepped into the real world.

## Bible Anchor

**Luke 2:1–7 (ESV)**

> In those days a decree went out from Caesar Augustus that all the world should be registered. This was the first registration when Quirinius was governor of Syria. And all went to be registered, each to his own town. And Joseph also went up from Galilee, from the town of Nazareth, to Judea, to the city of David, which is called Bethlehem, because he was of the house and lineage of David, to be registered with Mary, his betrothed, who was with child. And while they were there, the time came for her to give birth. And she gave birth to her firstborn son and wrapped him in swaddling cloths and laid him in a manger, because there was no place for them in the inn.

Luke anchors Jesus’ birth in history. He names Caesar Augustus, Quirinius, Syria, Galilee, Nazareth, Judea, Bethlehem, Joseph, Mary, and David’s family line. Those details matter because Christianity is not built on “once upon a time.”

**John 1:14 (ESV)**

> And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory, glory as of the only Son from the Father, full of grace and truth.

John teaches something even bigger than “Jesus existed.” **The Word** is a title for Jesus. **Became flesh** means the eternal Son of God became truly human. Jesus was not pretending to be a person. He entered our world with a real body, real hunger, real tears, real pain, and real love.

## Think It Through

Imagine someone says, “I do not believe Abraham Lincoln ever lived.” What would you do?

You might show them pictures, letters, speeches, government records, stories from people who knew him, and records from people who disagreed with him. One clue by itself might not answer everything, but many clues together make a strong case.

We do not have photos of Jesus, but we do have early writings, named places, public preaching, eyewitness testimony, and even evidence from people who were not Christians. The wise conclusion is simple: Jesus really lived.

Then we must ask the next question: If Jesus really lived, who is He?

## Honest Question

If Jesus really lived, why do some people still say He is only a legend?

## Kid-Friendly Answer

A **legend** is a story that grows bigger over time, sometimes adding made-up details. Some people call Jesus a legend because they do not want to believe the Bible, miracles, or Jesus’ claims about Himself.

But saying “legend” does not make the evidence disappear. The earliest Christians preached Jesus as a real person very soon after His life. They named people and places. They taught that Jesus was born, ate with people, taught crowds, was crucified under Roman authority, was buried, and rose again.

Christians should answer calmly. We do not need to mock anyone. We can say, “That is a serious question. Let’s look at the evidence.” The evidence points to Jesus as a real person in history, not a character invented long afterward.

## Interactive Activity

### Real Person Detective Board

**Supplies:**
- Index cards or sticky notes
- Pencil or marker
- A table, wall, or poster board

**Setup:**
Write one clue on each card:

- Real ruler: Caesar Augustus
- Real place: Bethlehem
- Real family: Joseph and Mary
- Real event: census registration
- Real witnesses: disciples and crowds
- Real opposition: leaders argued against Jesus
- Real public message: early Christians preached Jesus openly
- Real outside mentions: some non-Christian ancient writers mention Jesus or Christians

**How to play:**
1. Spread the cards out like detective clues.
2. Have students sort them into groups: “Bible details,” “public witnesses,” “opposition,” and “outside sources.”
3. Ask: “If Jesus were made up much later, which clues would be hard to explain?”
4. Let each student pick one clue and explain why it matters.

**Key idea:** Real history leaves tracks. The life of Jesus left tracks in Scripture, public witness, early Christian preaching, and ancient history.

## Parent / Teacher Discussion

Use these questions for a calm conversation:

1. What does the word “history” mean?
2. Why does Luke name rulers and places when he tells about Jesus’ birth?
3. Why is it helpful that both friends and enemies of Jesus treated Him as real?
4. What is the difference between saying “Jesus existed” and saying “Jesus is Lord”?
5. How can evidence help our faith without replacing faith?

Guide children away from two mistakes:

- **Mistake 1:** “If Jesus was historical, everyone will automatically trust Him.”  
  Better: History can show Jesus really lived, but each person must still respond to who He is.

- **Mistake 2:** “Questions are dangerous.”  
  Better: Honest questions can be brought into the light. Truth belongs to God, so Christians can seek it with humility and courage.

**If children ask about sources outside the Bible:**  
Explain that some ancient non-Christian writers mention Jesus or the early Christians. They usually do not tell the whole gospel, and they were not writing Scripture, but they help show that Jesus and the Christian movement were known in real history.

## Short Prayer
