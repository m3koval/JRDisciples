# Alone with God — source and production record

## Sermon-to-child connections

1. **God moves toward us first.** Romans 5:8; 1 John 4:19. Prayer responds to His love; it does not buy His attention or earn salvation.
2. **Speak to your Father personally.** Matthew 6:6 in the context of 6:5–8 contrasts sincere prayer with performing for people. Quiet space helps attention; locked doors and silence are not spiritual power sources. Corporate prayer remains good.
3. **Jesus made time for prayer.** Mark 1:35–38: early morning, solitary place, searching disciples, and His preaching mission. Luke 6:12–13: all-night prayer before choosing the twelve apostles. Do not assign an all-night prayer requirement to children.
4. **Learn to pray.** Luke 11:1 records a disciple's request. Do not expand this to the unsupported claim that the disciples never asked about miracles or healing.
5. **Depend on God throughout the day.** 1 Thessalonians 5:17; Ephesians 6:18. Prayer belongs to spiritual struggle and perseverance, not a formula that controls outcomes. Concrete child application: ask for help to tell the truth or respond kindly, then obey.
6. **Delight in God Himself.** Psalm 63:1,3 ESV corresponds to Psalm 62:2,4 Russian Synodal. Desire for God and joy in His faithful love, not merely a list of requested gifts.
